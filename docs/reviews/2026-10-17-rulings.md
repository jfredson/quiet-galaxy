## The items that matter most

Four items would change the headline by more than its noise, and one would change what the site says the biggest factor means:

1. **Item 2, raise the star-formation top from 10 to 20.** Recommended **accept**. The one accepted number change: the headline moves from 59% to 58% alone in the galaxy.
2. **Item 9, widen the spread of the chance life starts from 50 to at least 1,000 powers of ten.** Recommended **decline**. It would convert two authors' bounds on one chemical mechanism into half the weight of the headline's biggest factor, and the engine cannot compute it (see the note under the item).
3. **Item 12, lower the floor for the chance of intelligence from 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 10 billion.** Recommended **decline**, but it is the item where John could most reasonably differ: at 1 in 10 billion the chance we are alone in the galaxy goes from 59% to 77%.
4. **Item 15, lower the floor for the share that become detectable from 1 in 100 to 1 in 10,000.** Recommended **accept only if the 2020 estimate it rests on checks out**; it would take the galaxy figure from 59% to 65%.
5. **Item 7, lower the floor for habitable planets per star from 1 in 100 to 1 in 100,000.** Recommended **decline the number, accept the correction to the reason**: the record's own note already says the reason misdescribes the Rare Earth figure.

## Note for the method's own review (not an item)

The reviewer said in both the life and the signals packets that the chance we are alone in the galaxy should be the average of e to the minus N across the runs, not the share of runs with N below 1, "as the 2018 paper did". `docs/method.md` already covers this: the paper's main text reports the share below 1 (its 52%), its footnote 3 gives the exact figure and the difference of about one percentage point, and the site follows the paper's main text. Nothing to do now; the method's review can decide whether to report both.

A second note from running the scratch copies: the engine's draw for the log-normal rate overflows at a spread of about 80 powers of ten or more (item 9). Nothing in use is near it, but the validator does not refuse such a spread, so it should either refuse spreads the engine cannot draw or the draw should be rewritten to work in powers of ten.
