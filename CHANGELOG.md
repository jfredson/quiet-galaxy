- **First-pass ranges set**, all log-uniform, each with a written reason: stars per year 1 to 10 (narrower than the 2018 paper's 1 to 100; measurements cluster at 1.4 to 3.7); share with planets **raised from 0.1 to 0.5** at the floor, with a revision entry (no survey since 2011 finds fewer than half of stars with a planet); habitable planets per star 0.01 to 1; chance life starts one in ten to the 22 to 1 (one start per observable universe, as a stand-in for the 2018 paper's shape, which the record layout does not allow); intelligence 0.0001 to 1; detectable 0.01 to 1; lifetime 100 to ten billion years.
- **The headline is computed.** `scripts/compute.py run` plays the equation out 200,000 times with one independent draw per factor and writes `data/headline.json`. Given the 2018 paper's own ranges it reproduces their result: median 0.31 to 0.33 (theirs 0.32), 52% alone in the galaxy (theirs 52%), 39% alone in the observable universe (theirs 38%). With the first-pass ranges: **median about 2 in a billion detectable civilisations, 88% chance we are alone in the galaxy, 39% chance we are alone in the observable universe**; the most pessimistic published estimate of every factor at once gives one in ten to the 65, the most optimistic five trillion. The parameters (runs, seed, independent draws, galaxy count, where the result lives) and a third range shape for the chance life starts are proposed in `docs/method.md` with confidence and alternatives, **not ruled**; the method note shows the headline swings between 88% and 59% on that one shape.
